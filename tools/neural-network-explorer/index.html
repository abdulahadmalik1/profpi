What's actually happening

Each neuron computes a weighted sum of its inputs plus a bias — Σ(w·x) + b — then squashes it through an activation function. Stack enough of these and the network can bend a straight decision boundary into whatever shape the data needs. The output neuron uses a sigmoid, so its activation is a probability between 0 and 1.

How training works

The loss (cross-entropy) measures how wrong the prediction was. Backpropagation applies the chain rule layer by layer, starting at the output, to compute exactly how much each weight contributed to that error — the error signal δ. Gradient descent then nudges every weight a small step opposite its gradient, scaled by the learning rate, and repeats.

Reading the diagrams

In the network view, each node's number is its current activation for whatever point you're probing; edge color shows a weight's sign and thickness shows its magnitude. In the decision boundary, color is the model's live prediction across the whole plane — watch it reshape itself as training runs.
